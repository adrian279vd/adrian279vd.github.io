About my

I am a PhD candidate in Mathematics at Universidad de Talca, Chile, working under the supervision of Jan Felipe van Diejen. I was born in Cuba, where I completed my undergraduate studies. I initially focused on applied mathematics, particularly optimization and programming.

During my master's studies, my interests shifted toward spectral theory and orthogonal polynomials. This eventually led me to symmetric functions and their connections with mathematical physics, probability, and integrable systems, often in settings involving boundary interactions.

Outside mathematics, I enjoy football, hiking, video games, and spending time in quiet places.

Current Research

My research is centered on symmetric functions, orthogonal polynomials, integrable systems, and spectral theory, together with their connections to probability and mathematical physics. I am particularly interested in settings where explicit algebraic and special-function structures can be used to study spectral and stochastic problems.

A significant part of my work concerns orthogonal polynomials arising from degenerations and special cases of the Koornwinder framework. In particular, I have worked with Askey–Wilson polynomials and with multivariable Hall–Littlewood-type polynomials associated with hyperoctahedral symmetry. I am interested in their analytic and algebraic properties, including orthogonality, zeros, explicit formulas, spectral interpretations, and the role of boundary parameters.

Another direction of my research involves exactly solvable and integrable models. I have studied spectral problems for quantum spin chains, where orthogonal polynomials and related special functions provide explicit descriptions of eigenvectors and spectral data. More broadly, I am interested in the interaction between integrable systems, difference operators, and orthogonal polynomial structures.

More recently, I have been exploring probabilistic models on partitions and related discrete state spaces. This includes random walks and Markov chains whose transition operators admit explicit spectral descriptions through symmetric or orthogonal polynomials. I am particularly interested in the interplay between algebraic structure, boundary effects, invariant measures, and long-term stochastic behavior.

Across these directions, a recurring theme in my work is the use of explicit symmetric-function and special-function techniques to connect algebraic combinatorics, spectral analysis, mathematical physics, and probability.
